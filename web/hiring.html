Hiring 2026

People

Hiring has not stopped. Attention has. A cold application converts at about 0.5%. A referral converts at about 40%. Use AI to sharpen a specific application. Do not use it to spray them.

The plan

A written daily plan raised completed applications 15%, offers 30%, and shortened the search 26%. Do these three cycles. The briefing below is why each step is on the list.

Daily

One sitting

  1. Company sites from the last 72 hours. Open the companies you already track and keep roles posted in that window. Company sites still produce 52% of hires, and 40–60% of those applications are reviewed in the first three days.
  2. One message to a person. Use People. A direct note is read 60–80% of the time and becomes an interview 12–25% of the time.
  3. One application, on the company site. Tailor it. Stay at three subscribed openings per company. The yellow tag is the cap. A red tag means exclude one or change its status before you add another.
  4. Leave Easy Apply and auto-apply closed. Easy Apply converts at 2–4%. Mass apply converts at 0.1%.

Weekly

One block

  1. One pass on the niche boards. Wellfound, Built In, and Arc, plus the Brazil tech boards in Portals. Import the IT titles. Those boards run 30–80 applicants and 8–15% to interview.
  2. Two referral asks. Referrals are about 1% of applications and 35–40% of hires, with a 40–52% chance of a screen.
  3. Clear the stale and the red tags. Subscribed openings older than 30 days, and any company over three, get excluded or moved off subscribed.
  4. Add one thing a stranger can check. A number, a repo, or a published note tied to the roles you sent that week.

Monthly

One review

  1. Count interviews, not applications. Hours spent clicking submit predict early activity, not the offer.
  2. Fix the stage that stalled. No screens: change the target list or the resume. Screens that die in the panel: practice the live answer.
  3. Rewrite next month on one page. Titles, sectors, and work mode. That page is the written plan behind the 15%, 30%, and 26% figures.
  4. Keep the people who replied. Drop the portals that only added volume. Aggregator listings are 18–33% ghost jobs.

Ghost jobs

18–33% of listings on major aggregators have no funded hire behind them. Up to 40% of employers keep at least one. Hires per posting fell from 0.8 in 2019 to 0.4 in 2026.

  • Open longer than 30–45 days
  • Same text reposted across quarters
  • No specific team deliverable
  • Evergreen pipeline, budget pending, compliance, or a signal to investors

Auto-apply claims versus what happened

  1. 127,964 attempts
  2. 58,328 reached a live form
  3. 66.2% of those forms were submitted
  4. ~33.8% stalled on layout, CAPTCHA, or missing answers
  5. 0.1% became an interview

Indeed paused its “Apply For Me” beta in August 2026. Employers were flooded with applicants who did not show up or did not fit.

  • Volume

    “Apply to 1,000+ jobs a week while you sleep.”

    LinkedIn and Indeed prohibit bots. Rate limits suspend profiles.

  • Tailored resume

    “A 100% tailored resume for every ATS.”

    Keyword stuffing. Vector search treats it as a semantic anomaly.

  • Delivery

    “Flawless submission everywhere.”

    About a third of attempts stall or drop.

  • Intent

    “A bigger footprint raises your odds.”

    Several business units in a few minutes is marked as spam.

  • Interviews

    “Offers within 30 days.”

    About 0.1% interview rate, and a mark on that company’s candidate record.

The ATS is not silently rejecting 95% of resumes. Ashby, Lever, and Greenhouse do not auto-reject on a match score unless the employer set a legal or certification knockout. On an 800-applicant role, a recruiter reads about 30–50 profiles, then stops. The miss is attention, not a hidden score.

Six-step loop

Hours spent clicking submit predict early activity, not the offer or the pay. A written daily plan raised completed applications 15%, offers 30%, and shortened the search 26%.

  1. 1 · Direction Name the titles, sectors, and work mode.

    Broad categories waste the week. Write the daily actions down.

  2. 2 · Proof Show an artifact a third party can check.

    Repo, design system, published note, or a scorecard. Generated prose is not proof.

  3. 3 · Access Find a person before the form.

    Alumni, a second-degree, or someone on the team. That route is the 40–52% screen.

  4. 4 · Apply Company site, inside 72 hours.

    Single column, ordinary type, dates in order. No graphics or tables in the file.

  5. 5 · Convert STAR, out loud.

    Panels and transcription tools both need a situation, a task, an action, and a result.

  6. 6 · Learn Read the stage that stalled.

    No screens: targeting or the resume. Panels that die: the live answer, not another hundred applies.

How AI still helps

Use it

  • Pull the real problem and the team’s vocabulary out of a posting.
  • Turn a result you already have into a number a scorecard can read.
  • Rehearse a hard behavioral question before the panel.

Leave it

  • Autonomous form fillers and “apply for me” agents.
  • Hidden prompt text aimed at the ATS.
  • A letter or answer you did not read before it was sent.
Briefing notes from the October 2026 report

The market is congested at the top and slow underneath. Openings sit near 6.9 million against 5.6 million monthly hires. Quits are 3.2 million and layoffs 1.9 million. Employers are reluctant both to let people go and to add seats, so a search now runs for months. Mean unemployment is about 24 weeks.

Applying became nearly free once chatbots and auto-apply extensions spread, so volume outran human review. Across 109 million applications and 247,000 openings, applications per hire tripled from 2021 to 2024, stayed above 300 through 2025, and averaged 291 in early 2026. A candidate is about half as likely to get an interview as five years ago. Business roles now take 11.7 interview interactions and about 8 weeks. Technical roles take 17.6 interactions and about 10 weeks, partly to check AI-inflated claims. The failure is signal, not a freeze in hiring.

Company career sites, on Ashby, Greenhouse, Workday, and the rest, still account for 52% of hires, up from 38% in early 2021, because the resume lands intact. They are also the pile-up: hundreds of applications in week one, and a weaker look after 72 hours. LinkedIn is where 71–95% of recruiters search. Easy Apply is the low-yield door. A direct note to the person who owns the work is the high-yield door. Niche boards cut the crowd to dozens and improve time-to-fill 16–49% for the employer.

Referrals are about 1% of applications and 35–40% of hires. Cold inbound is 93.8% of applications, converts to an interview at 3–3.5%, and still produces 52% of hires only because of its mass. Referred hires hit the top performance rating 36% of the time versus 28% for cold hires, stay a median 38 months versus 22, and remain at 45% after two years versus 20% for job-board hires. That is why recruiters read them first.

Direct enterprise portals, used early and on purpose, remain the inbound base. LinkedIn is the directory and the outbound channel. Niche boards cover a specific craft. The advantage in 2026 is a checked record, a narrow target, and a person who will walk the file in.
